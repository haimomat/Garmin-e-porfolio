2. THE LADDER OF ENGAGEMENT

Introduction to the Framework

Customer engagement in the digital era is not a monolith; it exists on a spectrum. The "Ladder of Engagement," introduced by PR Smith, illustrates the different levels of customer interaction, progressing from passive observation to active collaboration (Smith, 2020; Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). The levels typically ascend through Ratings, Reviews, Discussions, Ideas, and finally, Collaborative Co-Creation. Moving customers up this ladder turns them into powerful brand advocates (Hollebeek, Glynn and Brodie, 2014).

Garmin Forerunner: Climbing the Ladder

Garmin, specifically through its Forerunner series targeted at runners and triathletes, brilliantly executes strategies across all levels of this ladder:

  • Ratings & Reviews: At the base level, Garmin encourages users to leave product ratings and detailed reviews on its official website and through retail partners to build initial trust (Tiago and Veríssimo, 2014).
  • Discussions (and Sharing): Garmin elevates engagement through its proprietary app, Garmin Connect. Here, users sync their Forerunner data, share running metrics, and discuss training plans with connections. Active communities on platforms like Reddit (r/Garmin) serve as hubs for organic brand discussions (Muniz and O'guinn, 2001).
  • Ideas: Garmin actively crowdsources innovation through its "Garmin Ideas" submission portal, where users can directly suggest software updates or new metrics they want to see in future Forerunner models (Kannan and Li, 2017).
  • Collaborative Co-Creation: The pinnacle of Garmin's engagement is the Connect IQ Store. Garmin opened its API to allow independent developers and tech-savvy users to build, code, and share custom watch faces, data fields, and widgets (Garmin Ltd., 2025). This open-source approach not only enhances the product's functionality but allows the top 1% of users to actively co-create the Garmin software ecosystem.

3. EVALUATION OF A SOCIAL MEDIA PLATFORM

Figure 3.1: Strava Platform Overview
Figure 3.2: Four Zones of Social Media Framework

To evaluate Strava—the primary social network for Garmin users—we can apply the "Four Zones of Social Media" framework proposed by Tuten and Solomon (2017), which divides social media into Social Community, Social Publishing, Social Entertainment, and Social Commerce. According to Kaplan and Haenlein (2010), understanding these functional building blocks is crucial for modern brand strategy.

  • Social Community: Strava’s core is built on relationships. Users connect by joining local running "Clubs," leaving comments, and giving "Kudos" (Strava’s equivalent of a 'Like') on workout activities (Kietzmann et al., 2011). It acts as a digital running club for Garmin users.
  • Social Publishing: When a user finishes a run with their Garmin Forerunner, the biometric data is automatically published to Strava. Furthermore, Strava integrates seamlessly with Instagram, allowing users to publish their route maps directly to their Instagram Stories as user-generated content (Stephen, 2016).
  • Social Entertainment: Strava gamifies fitness. Features like "Segments" (virtual race sections on real-world roads), "Leaderboards," and "Local Legends" create a highly competitive and entertaining environment. Users even engage in "GPS Art," running specific routes to draw pictures on the map (Ryan, 2016).
  • Social Commerce: Brands leverage Strava's commerce zone by hosting "Sponsored Challenges." For example, a sportswear brand might challenge users to run 50km in a month. Upon completion, users unlock a digital badge and a discount code for e-commerce purchases, driving direct sales (Lamberton and Stephen, 2016).

A business website must prioritize customer acquisition, conversion, and retention (Chaffey and Smith, 2017). Given the highly technical nature of smartwatches, a website checklist was created based on standard digital marketing criteria to evaluate Garmin's official e-commerce site (Garmin.com).

Garmin.com Evaluation Breakdown

As illustrated in the evaluation scores above, Garmin.com scores exceptionally well across crucial digital touchpoints:

  • High-Quality Content & Proper Domain (10/10): The site features 3D interactive product models, detailed algorithm explanations (e.g., how VO2 Max is calculated), and operates on a secure, localized .com domain (Kotler, Kartajaya and Setiawan, 2016).
  • Accessible Navigation & Mobile Design (9/10): The mega-menu brilliantly categorizes a massive product line (Automotive, Aviation, Marine, Fitness), making user journeys highly intuitive even on responsive mobile layouts.
  • Blog/Review & Contact Info (9/10): The "Garmin Blog" provides rich content on training tips and software update patch notes, while the "Support Center" offers highly visible live chat and repair contacts.
  • Fast Loading Times & Social Media Integration (8/10): Despite heavy multimedia usage, the site relies on effective content delivery networks (CDNs) to maintain speed, with clear links to social communities in the footer (Dodson, 2016).

5. TREND ANALYSIS

A defining trend shaping the digital marketing landscape in the 2020s is Wearable Tech Data Personalization fueling User-Generated Content (UGC).

Figure 5.1 (Figure 2 in text): Strava Global User Growth (2021-2025)

The Trend

Consumers no longer purchase smartwatches merely for hardware; they buy them for personalized algorithmic insights. Modern devices collect millions of data points, which brands translate into highly personalized daily metrics. Concurrently, the desire to share personal health achievements online has skyrocketed, as evidenced by the massive user growth of fitness platforms like Strava shown in Figure 2 (Strava, 2024; Statista, 2024).

Marketer's Perspective & Application

From a digital marketer's viewpoint, this trend represents a goldmine for authentic brand advocacy (MacInnis, Park and Priester, 2014). Brands like Garmin take advantage of this by transforming complex biometric data into easily digestible, highly shareable visual metrics—such as the "Body Battery," "Sleep Score," or "Daily Suggested Workouts."

Figure 5.2: Garmin Connect Visual Metrics
Figure 5.3: User-Generated Content on Social Media

Instead of spending heavily on traditional paid media, marketers engineer shareability directly into the UI/UX of the companion apps (We Are Social & Hootsuite, 2024). When a runner achieves a new VO2 Max, the Garmin Connect app generates a sleek, branded graphic overlaying their statistics onto a personal photo. The user then proudly shares this UGC on Instagram or Strava.

This strategy capitalizes on the psychological phenomenon of social validation. Every shared graphic acts as a micro-endorsement, proving the device's accuracy and value to the user's network (Zook and Smith, 2016). For brands, taking advantage of this trend requires a seamless integration between data science (to provide accurate insights) and digital marketing (to make those insights frictionless to share).
